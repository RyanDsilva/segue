Segue for Mac

Your chart, your set, your cue.

Lyrics, chords and setlists for musicians who play live. Built for a MacBook on a music stand.

Coming soon to the Mac App Store.

Free, with no ads and no in-app purchases. Requires a Mac with Apple silicon and macOS 26 or later.

How it works

From a pile of chord sheets to a Friday night set.

  1. Screenshot placeholder

    Monday

    Get your songs in.

    Drag ChordPro or text files onto your library, or start a new song from scratch. Each song is a plain file in a folder you choose, so it opens in any ChordPro app and backs up like everything else on your Mac.

  2. Screenshot placeholder

    Tuesday

    Make the chart yours.

    Type ChordPro directly, or switch to the visual editor and drop a chord right onto the syllable. Transpose in one step, add a capo, or flip to Nashville numbers when the singer changes key at the last minute.

  3. Screenshot placeholder

    Thursday

    Build the set.

    Drag songs into tonight's order and see the running time add up. Change a song's key or capo for this show only, without touching the song itself.

  4. Screenshot placeholder

    Friday

    Play.

    Start the show and Segue goes dark and full screen. Every song fits the screen at a size you can read from a stool, and what's next is always at the bottom.

Stage mode

Built for the stage, not the desk.

The stage view has one job: show you the right words at the right moment, and stay out of your way.

Screenshot placeholder
  1. Never too small to read.Set a minimum lyric size. If a song can't fit at that size, Segue scrolls instead of shrinking it.
  2. Know what's next.The footer shows the next song with its key and capo. When someone requests a song, it takes that spot.
  3. Take requests mid-set.Press Cmd-K, type a few letters of a title or a line you remember, and play it now, next, or later.
  4. Hands stay on the instrument.Arrow and Page keys move through the set, so page-turner pedals that send those keys work out of the box.
  5. Back where you were.If your Mac restarts or Segue quits mid-gig, it reopens at the song you were on.
  6. A quiet Mac.Run your own Shortcut when the show starts, for example to turn on a Focus, and a short checklist reminds you about the charger and brightness.

The details, done properly.

  • Your songs, as files.

    One ChordPro file per song in a folder you choose. No account, no sign-in, no lock-in.

  • Search the way you spell.

    Find a song by any half-remembered line, including romanized Hindi lyrics typed the way people actually type them.

  • Transpose, capo, Nashville.

    Shapes or concert pitch, any key, one click.

  • Guitar and piano diagrams.

    Chord diagrams for guitar, with your own shapes saved in the song, plus piano voicings.

  • Learning, Ready, Pro.

    Tags and proficiency marks show at a glance what's gig-ready.

  • Nothing to save.

    Every edit is saved as you go, and deleted songs go to the Trash.

  • At home in Finder.

    Open .cho files with Segue, preview them with Quick Look, and export your whole library as one archive.

  • Keyboard first.

    Every command has a key. Press ? to see them all.

Privacy

Your songs stay yours.

No account and no sign-in. Your songs, setlists and gig history live in files on your Mac, and Segue never uploads them. The first time you open Segue, it asks two separate questions: whether to share usage statistics, and whether to send crash reports. The choices are equal, nothing is picked for you, and you can change your mind any time in Settings > Privacy. Neither ever includes your songs. Bug reports go out only when you press Send, after you've seen everything in them. No ads, and no tracking.
